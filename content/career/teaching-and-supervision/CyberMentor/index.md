> [CyberMentor](https://cybermentor.de/) Germany’s largest research-based online mentoring programme for girls in STEM, run since 2005 at the University of Regensburg. Pupils in school years 5 to 13 are paired one-to-one with mentors from STEM professions for at least one year; up to 800 pairs per year, more than 11,000 since 2005.  Its aim is to carry an interest once awakened across the critical transitions of a school career.


CyberMentor: mentor since April 2024; design and delivery of my own multi-session project Workshops

2026: It started with two thirteen-year-olds. As a mentor in CyberMentor, I wanted to show them something from my own work that would be of use to them, so I asked what actually excites them and offered three ideas as inspiration.
The project became a book nook. I built it first myself, with open-source tools, 3D printing, an ESP32, an infrared motion sensor and LEDs, and then worked it into steps they could follow. Engineering, open hardware and the
physics underneath came with it, and it worked entirely remotely. I prepared the physics as questions rather than explanations, because they are thirteen. What does the white cap on the motion sensor do? One answer was protection. It is a Fresnel lens, and the sensor behind it does not see people at all, only changes in thermal radiation, which is why it cannot tell a cat from a person. That limit is where LWIR cameras begin. 

> A book nook is an illuminated miniature diorama placed in the gap between two books on a shelf, made to look as though it leads into another world.
