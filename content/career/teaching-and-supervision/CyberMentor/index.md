[CyberMentor](https://cybermentor.de/) is Germany's largest research-based online mentoring programme for girls in STEM, run by the University of Regensburg since 2005. Pupils in school years 5 to 13 are paired one-to-one with a mentor from a STEM profession for at least a year. The aim is to carry an interest, once awakened, across the transitions of a school career where it usually gets lost.

I have been a mentor since April 2024. Alongside the one-to-one mentoring, I design and run my own project workshops over several sessions.

## The book nook

It started with two thirteen-year-olds. I wanted to show them something from my own work that would be of use to them, so I asked what actually excites them and offered three ideas as inspiration.

The project became a book nook: an illuminated miniature diorama that sits in the gap between two books on a shelf and looks as though it leads into another world. I built it first myself, with open-source tools, 3D printing, an ESP32, an infrared motion sensor and LEDs, and then worked it into steps they could follow. Engineering, open hardware and the physics underneath came with it, and it worked entirely remotely.

I prepared the physics as questions rather than explanations, because they are thirteen. What does the white cap on the motion sensor do? One answer was: protection. It is a Fresnel lens. The sensor behind it does not see people at all, only changes in thermal radiation, which is why it cannot tell a cat from a person. That limit is exactly where the LWIR cameras from my own work begin.
